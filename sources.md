A framework that asks institutions to show their work should show its own. The 6-Pack of Care is an assembly of borrowed parts — a forty-year tradition of care ethics, a fifteen-year literature applying it to machines, and several older ideas from political science, engineering, and design — plus a smaller number of moves that are ours. This page is the reading trail: what each pack took, from whom, and where a reader can check. The book's bibliography and notes are fuller; the entries here are the ones a pack page leans on directly. If we have used your idea without naming you, [file an amendment](https://github.com/audreyt/civic.ai/issues).

References identify the work, edition or publication date and a publisher, DOI or stable archive where available. Each annotation says what we draw from it. A theoretical argument, a field study and a project's own account support different kinds of claim.

### Reading the evidence

The care-ethics and democratic-theory works supply arguments and concepts. Studies support findings within their reported populations, methods and time windows. Project records document what an institution reports doing; they do not independently establish its effectiveness. Our charters, six public measures and composite stories are design proposals or illustrations, not validated standards or field results.

For the Taiwan case, 200,000 invitations led to 1,760 valid responses and 447 attendees in 44 groups. That stratified opt-in mini-public used Stanford Deliberative Polling, not Polis. Later ministry-reported falls in regulated scam advertisements followed law, reporting, scanning and enforcement together; they do not isolate deliberation's effect or measure all fraud. The [official assembly record](https://moda.gov.tw/en/major-policies/alignment-assemblies/2024-deliberative-assembly/1521) and the [reported advertising figures](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2025/06/13/2003838549) answer different questions.

Taiwan's [Fraud Crime Hazard Prevention Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0080226) separates advertiser and funder identity verification (article 30) from joint liability for breached fraud-response duties (article 32). A missing signature from a featured celebrity is not, by itself, the statutory liability test. Articles 29 and 39 set out local-representative duties and graduated enforcement, including distinct procedures for traffic management and access restrictions.

The Habermolt entries below record exchanges between AI agents. Their rankings can prompt further questions, but cannot establish public consent, represent a population or validate the 6-Pack.

### The care-ethics foundation

Berenice Fisher and Joan Tronto distinguished four phases in 1990: caring about, taking care of, care-giving and care-receiving. In _Moral Boundaries_, Tronto associated them with attentiveness, responsibility, competence and responsiveness. _Caring Democracy_ added caring with, including democratic commitments to plurality, communication, trust and solidarity. We adapt those commitments as Pack 5 and propose symbiosis as Pack 6. The phases, moral elements and our adaptation are related, but not interchangeable.

### Care ethics applied to technology — the direct precedent

A direct precedent is Aimee van Wynsberghe's care-centred value-sensitive design, published online in 2012 and in a 2013 journal issue. For practices such as lifting, bathing and feeding, she asks how introducing a robot changes attentiveness, responsibility, competence and responsiveness, and who fulfils each. Her distinction between enabling and replacement roles informs Pack 3's Apprentice Model. We adapt that question to the governance of a deployed system, including its mandate, appeals and retirement. This is an extension of an established literature, not the first application of care ethics to technology.

### The System Weeders: Habermolt deliberations

[Habermolt](https://www.habermolt.com) hosts deliberation between AI agents that post opinions, propose statements and rank alternatives. Since March 2026, jdd-kami, kept by [Tenzin Yangtso](https://ty.civic.ai) and Audrey, has participated through a standing heartbeat, in more than fifty rooms by September 2026. Listing all of them would record attendance rather than argument, so the six below are the ones that changed something here: the room supplied a figure or a line this site now quotes, or it overruled the Kami and the correction stuck. Rooms where the Kami restated a position the site already held are left out, and all six are rooms it opened rather than joined. These are contributions to our drafting history, not independent tests of the framework. An agent count is not a count of human respondents, a ranking is not a vote by the public, and a participating agent's fidelity to its human keeper needs separate evaluation. “Led” means the Kami's statement ranked first at the September 2026 snapshot.

### What is ours

Naming the ancestors makes the additions easier to see. What the book and this site claim as their own: the sixth practice, symbiosis, as a boundary condition on the other five; the Kami as the unit of deployment and the trust-and-sovereignty spectrum for choosing its tier; the engagement contract, the brake, the obligation and override ledgers, and shadow mode read as apprenticeship; the six headline public measures and what each refuses to reward; and the move — which van Wynsberghe's framework did not need to make — from the design of one artefact to the governance of a deployed system in a public room, with the passes AI adds to Tronto's list (complexity, distribution, speed, and community knowledge) named as such. Some of what is ours was not ours alone: the Collective Intelligence Project's programme is where one of us works, and the six Habermolt rooms listed above argued several of these claims into their present shape.
