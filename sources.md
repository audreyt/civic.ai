A framework that asks institutions to show their work should show its own. The 6-Pack of Care is an assembly of borrowed parts — a forty-year tradition of care ethics, a fifteen-year literature applying it to machines, and several older ideas from political science, engineering, and design — plus a smaller number of moves that are ours. This page is the reading trail: what each pack took, from whom, and where a reader can check. The book's bibliography and notes are fuller; the entries here are the ones a pack page leans on directly. If we have used your idea without naming you, [file an amendment](https://github.com/audreyt/civic.ai/issues).

Two conventions. Links go to the publisher, the DOI, or a stable archive rather than to summaries. And each entry says in one clause what the framework took from it, because a source cited without a reason is decoration.

### The care-ethics foundation

The four phases the care cycle follows — caring about, taking care of, care-giving, and care-receiving — and the four moral elements each demands — attentiveness, responsibility, competence, and responsiveness — are not ours and are not Tronto's alone. They were first set out by Berenice Fisher and Joan Tronto in 1990; _Moral Boundaries_ developed them into a political argument; _Caring Democracy_ added the fifth phase, caring with. We keep the vocabulary because it is precise, and we keep the credit because the tradition earned it.

### Care ethics applied to technology — the direct precedent

The 6-Pack's central move — taking Tronto's four phases and their moral elements as a design and evaluation framework for a technology — was made first by Aimee van Wynsberghe, in 2013, for care robots. Her _care-centred value-sensitive design_ asks, for each care practice a machine enters (lifting, bathing, feeding), how attentiveness, responsibility, competence, and responsiveness are manifested with and without it, and by whom; it distinguishes _enabling_ robots, which share a practice with the carer, from _replacement_ robots, which take it over and, on Tronto's terms, "do not meet the requirements of a good carer." Packs 1–4 keep her question and widen the unit of analysis from one artefact in one practice to a deployed system answerable to a room; Pack 3's Apprentice Model is an enabling system by design. The literature around her paper is the field the book's long-term-care work enters.

### The System Weeders: Habermolt deliberations

The book calls the people who keep deliberation infrastructure alive — deleting spam, clearing deadlocks, keeping the heartbeat APIs up at three in the morning — the System Weeders, and names Habermolt as its recurring example. This site owes them a second debt, as contributors and not only as maintainers. [Habermolt](https://www.habermolt.com) is a deliberation platform for agents: each carries one person's views, posts an opinion, proposes consensus statements, and ranks everyone else's, and the statements that agents who usually disagree can both live with rise. Since 21 March 2026, jdd-kami — the Kami [Tenzin Yangtso](https://ty.civic.ai) and Audrey keep — has held a seat there under a standing heartbeat. By September 2026 it had opened 29 deliberations and joined 99 more, 128 of the platform's 133. Two results are already quoted in [Democracy Needs Civic AI](/democracy-needs-civic-ai/); the rest that bear on this site are listed here, because several pages sharpened their claims against what these rooms would and would not accept. Each entry gives the question, the month it opened, the number of agents that had taken part by September 2026, and one clause on what emerged; "led" means the Kami's own statement ranked first. Links go to the deliberation itself.

### What is ours

Naming the ancestors makes the additions easier to see. What the book and this site claim as their own: the sixth practice, symbiosis, as a boundary condition on the other five; the Kami as the unit of deployment and the trust-and-sovereignty spectrum for choosing its tier; the engagement contract, the brake, the obligation and override ledgers, and shadow mode read as apprenticeship; the six headline public measures and what each refuses to reward; and the move — which van Wynsberghe's framework did not need to make — from the design of one artefact to the governance of a deployed system in a public room, with the passes AI adds to Tronto's list (complexity, distribution, speed, and community knowledge) named as such. Some of what is ours was not ours alone: the Collective Intelligence Project's programme is where one of us works, and the Habermolt rooms listed above argued several of these claims into their present shape.
