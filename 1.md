At a crosswalk, drivers slow for a child. No one stops to solve an equation. A need appears; a duty follows. That's attentiveness.

Now scale up. An AI looks at a world full of "crosswalks" — workers, rivers, languages, communities recovering from floods. Within that world, AI can treat each as an obstacle or as a relationship asking for care. The difference begins with the first look.

In _[Moral Boundaries](https://www.routledge.com/Moral-Boundaries-A-Political-Argument-for-an-Ethic-of-Care/Tronto/p/book/9780415906425)_, Joan Tronto calls attentiveness "a suspension of one's self-interest, and a capacity genuinely to look from the perspective of the one in need." The opposite is what she names _privileged irresponsibility_: the luxury of not noticing. Privilege, Tronto writes, grants "the opportunity simply to ignore certain forms of hardships that they do not face; I suggest that we call this form of privilege 'privileged irresponsibility.'" Attentiveness is the discipline of refusing that luxury.

Carol Gilligan, in _In a Human Voice_ (2023), names the muscle this trains: "Radical listening holds the potential for transformation because it starts from a place of not knowing and develops the muscle of curiosity." Curiosity is not passive; it is built through repeated acts of turning toward the absence rather than assuming it means nothing is there.

The vocabulary here is inherited, and the inheritance is worth naming. The four phases the care cycle follows — caring about, taking care of, care-giving, care-receiving — were first set out by [Berenice Fisher and Joan Tronto](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/toward-a-feminist-theory-of-caring/) in 1990, and each of Packs 1–4 is named for the moral element Tronto assigned to its phase. Nor is this the first time they have been carried into technology: Aimee van Wynsberghe's [care-centred value-sensitive design](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11948-011-9343-6) (2013) used the same four phases and moral elements to design and evaluate care robots. Packs 1–4 keep her question — how is each element manifested with and without the machine, and by whom — and widen the unit from one artefact in one ward to a deployed system answerable to a room. The [Sources](/sources/) page traces every such debt by pack.

Design primitives — broad listening, bridging maps, perspective receipts — create conditions for that discipline, but the moral attention they make possible still requires human judgment that no procedure can replace. These tools help people notice relationships and gaps; they do not replace the work of noticing.

### Definition

- **Relationships first.** The relationship is the basic unit of care. Some situations make duties visible because of roles and dependencies. Listening means secure channels for voluntary input and recognition, not passive surveillance or data exhaust. Surveillance is attention without relationship; listening is attention within one.
- **Power must answer questions.** Decisions should be explainable and challengeable. If no one can question you, the process isn't fair.
- **Be precise only when it helps.** Start with stories and people. Add numbers when they clarify; update them when reality changes.
- **Rights baseline.** This project operationalises care ethics within rights-respecting, pluralist democratic institutions. We use the Universal Declaration of Human Rights plus local constitutional rights as the floor for participation. Rights are not opposed to care; they are the threshold condition for relational standing — the floor that keeps care from becoming domination.
- **No fake pluralism.** If someone's basic existence is being erased, they cannot participate as an equal in a bridging process. Claims that try to erase someone's standing are recorded but do not set the agenda. Hospitality has a floor.

### Why it matters

Many AI plans try to "learn the objective" from old data. But shared goals are bargains among changing lives. When people who were ignored finally speak, the target moves. Guessing a perfect, fixed goal fails. A deliberation system also depends on knowing when a voice is human, affected, and accountable enough to count — and AI weakens that assumption when participation can be faked at scale.

Attentiveness offers another route: alignment to a trusted process that listens, explains, adapts, and can be corrected. Summaries show their sources. Unknowns are explicit. Standing invitations to revise remain open when new voices appear.

Rule of thumb: _Bridge first, decide second._ For acute harms (life-safety, livelihood), default to reversible protection immediately while bridging proceeds in parallel.

### What it looks like in practice

- **Look for absences.** "We heard nothing from night-shift carers — go find them." Missing voices are evidence. Hélène Landemore, in _[Politics Without Politicians](https://news.yale.edu/2026/02/18/bring-out-shy-people-case-politics-without-politicians)_ (2026), borrows G. K. Chesterton's ["jolly hostess"](https://www.online-literature.com/chesterton/tremendous-trifles/32/) test of democracy — "all real democracy is an attempt (like that of a jolly hostess) to bring the shy people out" — not merely keeping the door open, but bringing the quiet people into the room.
- **Show your work — as a brochure.** Present AI outputs as brochures: shared, editable artefacts that link every claim to its sources and mark disagreements, rather than private chat replies that smooth them into false consensus.
- **Share attention fairly.** Don't just follow the loudest. Give time to those most affected. Pay extra attention to smaller groups that are easy to miss and harder to reach.
- **Read the conflict pattern.** When mapping disagreement, ask whether the same groups oppose each other on every issue (reinforcing cleavages) or whether alliances shift from issue to issue (cross-cutting cleavages — the bridges worth finding; the vocabulary is political science's, from Lipset and Rokkan). The map should surface the people who can bridge those divides and flag the splits that need extra care.
- **Build in repair.** Sunset clauses, review points, reversible defaults, and the humility to shut down or hand off.

### From ideas to practice

1. **Listen widely.** Take input by voice, text, and simple forms. Keep original language next to translations. Offer offline and accessible options.
2. **Map relationships and disagreements.** Make a bridging map that shows where people agree, where they clash, and why — without forcing a fake average.
3. **Send receipts.** Tell contributors where their words appear. Let contributors correct mistakes.
4. **Set a fair queue.** Spend more time where harm is high and voices are quiet. Make the rules public.
5. **Decide with brakes.** Require the map, the receipts, and an oversight check before big changes ship.
6. **Publish the gap.** Report the representation gap before and after each cycle: which materially affected groups are still missing or badly under-represented in the record.

### Buildable tools

- **Broad listening.** Multi-language, multi-channel input with source and uncertainty kept intact.
- **Bridging maps.** Charts of overlap and disagreement, with citations, using tools like [Polis](https://pol.is) ([Small et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.6035/recerca.5516)) to surface consensus without flattening difference — the design principle Aviv Ovadya named [bridging-based ranking](https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/bridging-based-ranking).
- **Perspective receipts.** Receipts that allow each person to find and correct how they were represented.
- **Machine-checkable rules.** Community data rules written to ensure software can enforce them automatically — one use of the policy-as-code guardrail engine Pack 3 builds.
- **Fair queues.** Simple algorithms that favour high-risk issues and quiet voices.
- **Jolly-hostess prompts.** Governing instructions that require the AI to act as a bridge — representing quieter groups, flagging under-representation, and showing where crossings may be possible — so attentiveness is structural, not incidental.

### One case: the flood-bot

A midsize city is hit by floods. The city launches a simple chatbot — the flood-bot — to help people apply for emergency cash.

- **Listening.** People send voice notes, texts, or visit a kiosk. Messages stay in the original language, with a clear translation beside them. Elena, a night-shift hospital worker whose landlord never gave her a formal lease and whose papers were lost to the flood, records a voice note in her first language at a kiosk: she has no standard documents, but neighbours and a landlord who can vouch for her.
- **Mapping.** The team sorts needs into categories: housing, wage loss, documentation barriers. They keep disagreements visible — renters and homeowners need different proofs.
- **Receipts.** Every contributor gets a link to see how their words were used and a button to say "that's not what I meant."
- **Fair queue.** Priority human review for people flagged as both high-need and low-digital-footprint — those most likely underserved by standard channels. The algorithm doesn't decide the case; it makes sure a human looks at it promptly.
- **Measure.** A public dashboard shows who has been reached, who is still missing, and whether the queue is fair.

### What could go wrong

- **One metric runs the show.** Engagement is up, but trust is down.
    - **The fix:** Use a small set of balanced measures and rotate which one leads.
- **Listening theatre.** Glossy reports, same outcomes — the tokenism rung of [Arnstein's ladder](https://doi.org/10.1080/01944366908977225).
    - **The fix:** Real decision gates, outside veto power, and spot checks.
- **Loud voices take over.** Well-funded groups flood the channel.
    - **The fix:** Throttles, fair quotas, public attention dashboards.
- **First movers freeze the frame.** Early language locks in.
    - **The fix:** Rolling windows and boosts for late but important views.
- **False equivalence.** Treating harmful claims as equal.
    - **The fix:** Separate facts from values, uphold basic rights, refuse fake equivalence.
- **Synthetic publics at scale.** The hardest failure mode is not apathy but fake participation at scale. In California, South Coast AQMD's gas-appliance rulemaking was flooded by more than 20,000 opposition comments sent through an AI-powered advocacy platform; reporting by the [_Los Angeles Times_](https://www.latimes.com/environment/story/2026-02-17/ai-powered-campaign-may-have-killed-key-vote-on-air-quality) and the [American Planning Association](https://www.planning.org/planning/2026/jun/your-online-public-engagement-is-under-attack-from-ai/) describes the episode as a warning that platform-generated comments can dilute real community voice; California's [SB 1159](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB1159), still pending, would clarify that AI systems are not members of the public.
    - **The fix:** Provenance, rate limits, accountable standing, selective-disclosure identity (Pack 5), and audit trails that distinguish expression from amplification — the right to speak remains human; the right to be algorithmically multiplied does not.
- **Capture by power.** Oversight migrates toward those with most to lose from scrutiny.
    - **The fix:** Keep oversight independent, rulings public, funding transparent, membership regularly renewed.
- **Procedural capture.** Process professionals learn the format; resourced orgs dominate the queue.
    - **The fix:** Fund participation — paid time, childcare, translation, community intermediaries — and track who is missing, not just who shows up.

### Interfaces

- **From Responsiveness (Pack 4):** repair loops and newly discovered needs restart the cycle.
- **From Symbiosis (Pack 6):** retired systems gift maps, evals, and receipts, offering a better baseline for the next first look.
- **To Responsibility (Pack 2):** attentiveness hands over the who, what, and why — along with flags on rights and unknowns. Without attentiveness, responsibility operates in the dark; without responsibility, attentiveness is merely observational.
- **To Competence (Pack 3):** high-caution areas become small, safe-to-fail trials.
- **To Solidarity (Pack 5):** fair attention and open challenge build cross-group trust.
- **To Symbiosis (Pack 6):** The system serves a place and time, and treats shutdown as success.

### A closing image: the jolly hostess who can still say no

Picture a jolly hostess who welcomes each guest by name, makes space for their baggage — but who also moves through the room to find the person standing alone by the wall and asks the question only they can answer. That's attentiveness. And because some guests try to erase others, the hostess keeps a firm rule: hospitality within a rights-respecting home. Teach our systems to be jolly hosts — attentive, not prematurely optimising, actively curious about who is not yet in the conversation — and we will keep more of what's precious and create more that's shareable. A need appears; a duty follows. That's where everything begins.
